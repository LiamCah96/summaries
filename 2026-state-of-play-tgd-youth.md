title: "State of play: the mental health of trans and gender diverse young people in the UK"
paper: "State of play: mental health, gender minority stress and affirmative experiences among trans and gender diverse young people in the United Kingdom"
journal: BMC Psychiatry
year: 2026
doi: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-026-08554-4
date: 2026-07-17
---

**What we wanted to know.** Trans and gender diverse (TGD) young people report far poorer mental health than their cisgender peers, but UK evidence has mostly come from small samples that treat TGD young people as one homogeneous group. We wanted a national picture: how common are depression, anxiety, self-harm and suicidality among TGD young people in the UK, how do stressors like discrimination and conversion practices relate to those outcomes, and do affirming experiences, such as accepting communities and respected pronouns, offer protection?

**What we did.** We analysed survey data from 5,856 TGD young people aged 13 to 24 across the UK, collected in 2023, making this the largest UK sample currently available. We modelled five outcomes (probable depression, probable anxiety, and past-year suicidal ideation, suicide attempts and self-harm) against gender minority stressors (discrimination, victimisation, exposure to conversion practices) and affirmative experiences (community acceptance, access to affirming spaces, support from family and friends, pronoun respect and more), adjusting for gender identity, ethnicity, disability and autism, socioeconomic indicators and age.

**What we found.** The headline numbers are stark: around two thirds of participants met thresholds for probable depression and reported past-year suicidal ideation or self-harm, and nearly a quarter reported a past-year suicide attempt. Stress exposures were common too: seven in ten reported discrimination and 13% had been exposed to conversion practices. Each stressor was independently associated with worse outcomes across the board, with conversion practice exposure showing the strongest association with suicide attempts. On the protective side, community acceptance and access to LGBTQ+ affirming spaces were consistently linked to better outcomes; individual supportive relationships helped less consistently, suggesting environments matter more than any single supportive person can compensate for.

**What it means.** This crisis is neither a mystery nor inevitable. The poor mental health of TGD young people in the UK tracks identifiable, changeable social conditions: exposure to discrimination, victimisation and conversion practices on one side, and the availability of genuinely affirming environments on the other. For policy, that points to concrete priorities: reducing harm, and building credible affirmation across schools, services and communities.

**What it doesn't mean.** The data are cross-sectional, so they show associations, not proven causes. Recruitment was through social media, so young people without safe online access are missing, the sample skewed white, and trans women were underrepresented. The data also come from 2023, a period of intense public debate about trans people in the UK, and the landscape has shifted since, so treat this as a rigorous baseline rather than a snapshot of today.
